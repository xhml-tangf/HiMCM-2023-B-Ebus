\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Electrifying a Metro Bus Fleet Without Reducing Service}
\end{center}

\textbf{Question and baseline.} We study conversion of TransLink's fixed-route bus service to fully electric operation by 2033 subject to preserving recovered fall-2023 departures and capacity proxies. Existing trolleybuses count as electric; ordinary renewal is the comparator.

\textbf{Model.} We specify trip, capacity, winter energy and charger constraints; the city inventory remains a proportional scenario without a joint dispatch certificate. Gross discounted cost is separated from payments after 0\%, 25\% or 50\% grants; emissions use a shared annual service ramp and explicit partial boundaries. A global Pareto frontier requires plant data that are absent, so we report a named decomposition and do not label infeasible points as an optimum.

\textbf{Observed result.} The recovered fall-2023 GTFS contains 54,497 trips in 25 service patterns. The representative weekday has 1,752 source-declared blocks and 1,087 simultaneous trips; annual fixed-route service is 5.58 million hours and 106.87 million km. A fuel- and stock-weighted tailpipe proxy gives 126--408 tonnes NO$_x$ per year. Vehicle identity, age, depot and site power remain unresolved.\cite{gtfs2023,budget2025,eea2023,prati2025,bcvanderhoof}

\textbf{Conditional inventory and cost scenario.} At a 300-km overnight-charge sensitivity, the generic non-deck case has 1,612 net new battery positions. The integrated class envelope purchases 1,647 route BEBs plus 262 one-for-one trolley renewals (1,909 electric vehicles), ends with 1,927 electric and zero conventional fixed-route assets, and costs CAD 4.71 billion versus CAD 2.69 billion for renewal plus operations ($-75.0\%$). The high class case is CAD 5.26 billion ($-95.5\%$), and the 0/25/50\% grant scores are $-75.0/-42.8/-10.5\%$. Its partial-production, operation and maintenance boundary yields 4.6--42.7\% emissions reduction. A 700-kWh double-decker leaves 17 of 57 duties short of one overnight charge; a 150-kWh shuttle leaves 49 of 101. London's 17-year renewal peak is 776 vehicles per year.\cite{fleet2020,playbook2024,budget2025,foothill2024,endera2023}

\textbf{Recommendation.} Start depot power and grid upgrades in 2024, preserve the recovered service patterns, and procure proven 40-/60-ft blocks first. Gate double-deck and shuttle conversion on in-route charging, all-electric winter tests and verified site capacity.

\textbf{Keywords:} electric bus; fleet replacement; service preservation; charging; life-cycle cost.

\section{Problem definition and study boundary}
The competition asks for environmental and financial consequences, a ten-year transition in three metropolitan areas, and a decision-maker letter.\cite{problem} We take 31 December 2023 as Vancouver's decision baseline and plan purchases and construction in 2024--2033. Two evidence layers are reported separately: observed 2023 counts define the service floor, while a retrospective 2025 fleet snapshot and later public project envelopes reconstruct missing class/age context and bound future costs. No realized later purchase is presented as a 2023 decision. Metro Vancouver is the primary case; its census metropolitan area had 2,642,825 residents in 2021, exceeding the problem's 500,000-person threshold.\cite{vancouvercensus} Chicago and London test transferability using their separately dated 2023 fleet snapshots; London's March audit is not treated as December stock.

Prior fleet models optimize electric vehicle count and mix jointly with charging and scheduling,\cite{rogge} use a multi-period purchase, salvage and demand-charge formulation,\cite{pelletier} or solve multi-depot time-window and recharge constraints.\cite{gkiotsalitis} This study connects those ideas to a recovered public timetable, a capacity proxy where the vehicle roster is absent, a normal-renewal comparator, and a separately stated emissions boundary. The result is also a data-validity test: a missing garage or winter record changes the status to \texttt{UNVERIFIED} rather than becoming a zero-cost assumption.

The problem's definition requires propulsion \emph{and accessories} to use a zero-emission electric source. Thus a battery bus with a diesel auxiliary heater is not fully electric in this study. TransLink's historical depot-BEB cost table assumes precisely such a heater at 0.02 L/km; its 1.4 kWh/km electricity figure cannot be carried over unaltered to an all-electric winter bus. Renewable diesel, renewable natural gas and hybrids do not satisfy the endpoint. Hydrogen fuel cells are treated as a separate zero-tailpipe option because eligibility would require a certified zero-emission hydrogen supply. Already electric trolleybuses satisfy the endpoint, provided their wired operation remains feasible. TransLink has a 2040 zero-emission target, making 2033 an accelerated counterfactual.\cite{fleet2020,playbook2024}

The decision unit is year $y$, bus class $k$, depot $d$, trip $i$, vehicle $v$ and charger interval $t$. Classes include standard, articulated, highway coach, double-deck, community shuttle and trolley vehicles. We preserve 2023 fixed-route coverage and do not offset electrification cost by reducing service.

The 2018 official service guidelines define peak load factor as average peak passengers divided by practical vehicle capacity. Their reference capacities are 24 (mini-bus), 47 (standard trolley), 50 (standard bus or coach), 70 (articulated trolley), and 75 (articulated bus). Dividing each positive 2023 time-bin load by its load factor reproduces one of these values in 8,705 of 9,371 cells; a further 344 cells give exactly 85, consistent with TransLink's separately described 80--86-seat double-deck vehicles. The reproducible cell-level calculation and every unmatched value are retained in the data supplement.\cite{guidelines2018,doubledeck2017,loadcsv}

Capacity must also be checked on the replacement vehicle. Current TransLink operating limits are 20 passengers for community shuttles, 77 for 40-ft buses and 120 for articulated buses; the 2018 planning values are lower and distinguish seating from practical capacity.\cite{events2026,guidelines2018} Manufacturer examples span 68 passengers for a Nova LFSe+, 88 for a current 40-ft Xcelsior CHARGE NG, 118 for the 2023 XE60 specification and 123 for the current XE60. \section{Fleet and service model}
\subsection{Decision variables and normal-renewal baseline}
Let $x_{ykda}$ be buses purchased into class $k$, depot $d$ and age cohort $a$; $r_{ykda}$ old vehicles retired from that cohort; $h_{ydq}$ charger positions of power type $q$; and $z_{iv}$ equal one when vehicle $v$ performs scheduled trip $i$. Let $c_{vt}$ denote charge energy and $e_{vt}$ battery energy. For ordinary renewal, age-expired conventional buses are replaced with conventional equivalents while the \emph{same calendar service pattern and passenger-capacity demand} is served. Electric candidates are compared with that feasible renewal path. Historical purchase expenditure is sunk; early retirement enters through foregone residual or sale value.

For each class and depot, stock obeys
\begin{equation}
 N^{p}_{y,k,d,a}=N^{p}_{y-1,k,d,a-1}+x^{p}_{y,k,d,a}-r^{p}_{y,k,d,a},\quad p\in\{\mathrm{electric,conventional,trolley}\}.
\end{equation}
No diesel, hybrid, CNG or hydrogen propulsion or non-electric accessory may operate on any fixed-route trip in 2033. Retirement and purchase are not constrained one-for-one: needed vehicle counts emerge from schedules and spare ratios. A short-range vehicle may force additional buses, chargers or drivers.

\subsection{Hard service and technical constraints}
For every calendar service pattern $p$, every scheduled trip $i\in I_p$ is retained at its route, direction and departure time. Multiple smaller buses may serve one departure when necessary, and each assigned class must be compatible:
\begin{align}
 \sum_{v\in V_i}z_{iv}&\geq 1 &&(i\in I),\\
 \sum_{v\in V_i}Q_vz_{iv}&\geq Q_i^0 &&(i\in I),\\
 \sum_{i\in I_{r,s,t}}\sum_{v\in V_i}Q_vz_{iv}
   &\geq Q^{0}_{r,s,t}. &&
\end{align}
The final expression denotes capacity available at the original route--direction peak; the trip-level inequality protects each departure. A smaller bus can replace a larger one only if an additional vehicle on that departure restores the capacity and both vehicles pass scheduling, energy and site checks. Accessible trips require accessible vehicles; depot/class spare stocks are protected. If demand growth is modeled, it is applied equally to baseline and electric cases.

The source-declared block is the second hard unit. For consecutive trips $i\prec j$ in block $b$, retained on one vehicle, $s_j-e_i\ge\tau^{road}_{ij}+t^{turn}_{ij}$. If splitting is necessary, both vehicles must be able to reach a documented exchange or charging site without moving a departure. In the fall feed, 879 of 1,752 weekday blocks interline more than one route; none of the 20,679 consecutive transfers has zero gap across more than 0.5 km, supporting GTFS blocks as duty hypotheses. Most long blocks cannot recharge midday without replacement or terminal charging; distance divided by range is only a screen, not a vehicle count.

As a separate \emph{calibration check}, the schedule covers 22,492 bus trips on the maximum service day, while the annual output should reproduce 5,575,772 fixed-route bus service hours, 106,868,324 service km and 5,715,791,401 capacity km within documented measurement differences. The capacity-km subtotal detects systematic undersupply, but cannot substitute for departure, block and site constraints.\cite{gtfs2023,budget2025}

The executed range screen uses
\begin{equation}
d_b^U=\sum_{i\in b}\ell_i^U,\qquad
n_{bk}^{(j)}=\left\lceil\frac{d_b^U}{R_k^{(j)}}\right\rceil,
\end{equation}
where $R_k^{(j)}$ is an assumed reliable range and $n_{bk}^{(j)}$ is the number of sequential duty requests. This is a conditional distance screen, not a vehicle-count bound. A route exchange, depot return or charger restriction can only increase the physical requirement, while sharing split duties between blocks may reduce it; the result is therefore not a purchase count.

\begin{samepage}
Each vehicle's assigned trips and charging intervals cannot overlap. Location-to-location deadhead times must fit between events, including departure from and return to its depot. Driving to or from service also removes a vehicle from the available depot spare count. For battery vehicles, energy follows
\begin{align}
 e_{v,t+1}
 &=e_{vt}+\eta_q c_{vt}
   -\epsilon_{vk}^{\rm traction,winter}(\ell_{it}+\ell^{\rm deadhead}_{vt})
   -E^{\rm accessories}_{vt},\\
 \underline{s}_vB_v&\le e_{vt}\le \bar{s}_vB_v.
\end{align}
\end{samepage}
Here $E^{\rm accessories}_{vt}$ includes all-electric cabin heating and other onboard equipment during trips, deadhead, charging and active layovers; the traction coefficient excludes that energy to prevent double counting. An unmeasured winter accessory load makes an electric schedule \texttt{UNVERIFIED}, not zero-energy. Battery capacity and passenger capacity are linked when battery weight reduces available seats or payload. Charger occupancy, power and opening year impose
\begin{equation}
 \sum_v u_{vdt}\le H_{dt},\quad \sum_v P_{vdt}\le P^{\rm grid}_{dt},\quad
 H_{dt}=0\ \mathrm{until\ commissioned}.
\end{equation}
Conventional baseline vehicles instead require a fuel budget, with jointly calibrated distance and elapsed-duty rates $a_v,b_v$ in a consistent fuel unit (L or kg):
\begin{equation}
 F_{v,t+1}=F_{vt}-a_v\ell_{vt}-b_v\Delta t,\qquad
 F_v^{\rm reserve}\le F_{vt}\le F_v^{\rm tank}.
\end{equation}
The implemented check assumes no refuelling within a duty; the time rate includes stationary consumption and must not duplicate the distance rate. Missing fuel data leave the normal-renewal comparator unverified. Such vehicles may serve intermediate years but cannot satisfy the strict 2033 endpoint.

Trolleybuses use a separate wired-network check. Each trip, deadhead and powered layover needs a certified connected path and an operating feeder. Concurrent demand must obey $\sum_{v\in V_{ft}}P_v\le P_f^{\rm firm}$, where firm power is available after other loads and reserve. The code conservatively reserves each vehicle's measured all-in peak draw on every feeder traversed for the entire moving segment; stationary accessories use their separate demand. A failure of this sufficient power certificate calls for finer feeder timing before declaring physical infeasibility. Missing path or feeder records leave existing trolley operation unverified, without assigning these vehicles fictitious batteries or buying their capacity again.

Depot bus parking capacity, charger footprint, charging efficiency, construction lead time and route-specific winter testing must also pass. A charger site may be the home depot or a route terminal, provided the location and travel-time checks pass. The accompanying validator checks the supplied trip, energy and site records, returning \texttt{UNVERIFIED} for missing essential inputs; it cannot certify a city-wide solution from time-bin averages.

\subsection{Explicit assumptions and validation}
Five assumptions define the evidence boundary. (A1) Every recovered 2023 fall departure and direction is a service obligation; the representative weekday scales fleet demand. (A2) The values 24, 47, 50, 70, 75 and 85 are capacity proxies; 47 and 70 become trolley-route proxies only through the independent 262-trolley count. (A3) Reliable range is a technology scenario, and the screen from 149 to 350 km is not a delivered-battery guarantee. (A4) The unverified 1,725-bus scenario and 18 assumed existing BEBs seed the central stock table, with 5--18 BEBs retained as a sensitivity. (A5) Public vendor and project envelopes span different nominal years; their 3\% result is price-mixed screening PV. Validation uses source-total reconciliation, immutable GTFS hashes, interval distances, an independent charging LP bound, zero-gap London MILPs, a 432,768-case named-cell reproduction, and 2033 stock identities. All scenario and endpoint values are regenerated by the local pipeline.

\subsection{Optimization and executed decomposition}
The full problem is
\begin{equation}
\min C^{\rm gross}\quad\text{s.t. }
\begin{aligned}
&\text{service, block and capacity;}\\
&\text{SOC, charger and feeder;}\\
&\text{spare, inventory and endpoint.}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
followed by the $\epsilon$-constraint family $\min C^{\rm gross}$ subject to $G\le\epsilon$ for the Pareto frontier. The station and depot register is insufficient for that global integer program. We therefore execute a documented decomposition: (i) audit every calendar service pattern and block; (ii) screen alternate reliable ranges; (iii) price a fixed-service accelerated replacement scenario; and (iv) compute matched production, operation and maintenance emissions. The validator classifies absent inputs as \texttt{UNVERIFIED}. This separated approach follows the joint fleet/scheduling logic of Rogge et al., the multi-year transition logic of Pelletier et al., and the multi-depot timing logic of Gkiotsalitis et al.\cite{rogge,pelletier,gkiotsalitis}

\section{Costs, funding and environmental accounting}
Let $K_y$ include bus and battery procurement (initial battery included in a quoted vehicle price), chargers, depot civil work, grid connection and distribution upgrades, construction/design, training and any extra buses. Let $O_y$ include electricity, fuel, maintenance, replacement batteries, drivers, demand charges and regular charging-site upkeep. Let $S_y$ be realized salvage and $R_{2033}$ the remaining value of vehicles and durable infrastructure at the horizon. A complete appraisal requires one price basis and a matching real or nominal discount rate. The executed result below uses a stated 3\% discount as scenario arithmetic on public envelopes spanning 2021--2026 nominal CAD; it is therefore called price-mixed screening PV, not a constant-dollar bid appraisal.
\begin{align}
 C^{\rm gross}
 &=\sum_{y=2024}^{2033}\frac{K_y+O_y-S_y}{(1+r)^{y-2023}}
 -\frac{R_{2033}}{(1+r)^{10}},\\
 \Delta C&=C^{\rm gross}_{\rm electric}-C^{\rm gross}_{\rm renewal}.
\end{align}
For grant rate $f\in\{0,0.25,0.50\}$ and eligible expenditures $E_y$, $C^{\rm agency}=C^{\rm gross}-\sum_y fE_y/(1+r)^{y-2023}$. A grant changes who pays, not gross buses, copper, electricity or labour. Local employment and procurement impacts require an additional regional economic model and are reported separately to avoid double counting.\cite{usdot}

The regional layer records direct local procurement \(L_y\), wages \(W_y\) and job-years \(J_y\). Any induced value added uses a regional input--output multiplier \(m_y\) on labour and supplier value added, \(V_y^{\rm local}=m_y(L_y+W_y)\), not on the entire gross construction outlay. Feasible charging capacity, reliability and terminal asset value enter the fleet model through served trips, avoided extra vehicles, bill savings and residual value. Construction spending is not counted again as a benefit.

Define a life-cycle greenhouse-gas inventory over equal service:
\begin{align}
 G={}&G_{\rm vehicle+battery}+G_{\rm infrastructure}\\
 &+\sum_y\left(G_{\rm fuel,y}+G_{\rm electricity,y}
 +G_{\rm maintenance,y}\right)-G_{\rm end\ of\ life}.
\end{align}
Electricity uses an explicitly selected grid-intensity scenario. British Columbia's 2023 integrated-grid factor is 0.0113 kg CO$_2$e/kWh; it is an annual average and should be varied against marginal-generation possibilities.\cite{bcgrid} On-route NO$_x$, particles, electricity use and battery-production impacts appear in separate columns rather than being concealed by a single carbon score. If compatible manufacturing/infrastructure factors are absent, the \emph{life-cycle} score is unavailable even when operating emissions can be estimated.

The requested scores are
\begin{equation}
 S_C=100\frac{C_{\rm renewal}-C_{\rm electric}}{C_{\rm renewal}},\qquad
 S_G=100\frac{G_{\rm renewal}-G_{\rm electric}}{G_{\rm renewal}}.
\end{equation}
Negative values remain negative. The economic score is based on \emph{gross} cost, with subsidized agency cost shown beside it; otherwise the 50\% grant would artificially change the societal merit ranking.

\section{Observed and conditional results}
\subsection{Fall 2023 block and range screen}
The recovered feed contains 54,497 bus trips, 25 calendar service patterns and 1,684,516 bus stop-time records after excluding rail, SeaBus and HandyDART. On 14 November 2023 it schedules 22,431 trips and 1,752 source-declared blocks. At the busiest instant, 1,087 trips are simultaneous. The largest service day, 8 September, has 22,492 trips in 1,756 blocks. These are planned duties, not an as-operated or vehicle registry.\cite{gtfs2023}

For energy screening, we classify blocks by the 2023 route--time capacity signature: 24, 47, 50, 70, 75 and 85 passenger-space proxies. The labels identify capacity only. Route blocks with 47/70 signatures are retained as a trolley-route proxy because the separate 2023 fleet count has 262 trolleys, but the GTFS contains no propulsion field. The 57 weekday 85-person double-deck blocks are excluded from the overnight-depot arithmetic. A 300-km scenario gives 1,741 sequential battery-block requests for 1,502 participating blocks, 239 extra requests (15.9\%). At the 149/150-km electric-heat band, the corresponding requirement is 2,444 requests (62.7\% more). Saturday and Sunday produce larger ratios because fewer blocks cover long weekend duties; the weekday total sets the selected fleet scale.

\subsection{Charging tariffs and the value of firm power}
The original 2023 budget assumes diesel at CAD 1.66/L; this is a planning price, not an actual invoice. Its 32.17-million-L operational volume includes a broader system boundary and is not treated as fixed-route bus consumption.\cite{budget2023} BC Hydro's April-2023 tariff supplies energy, monthly demand and daily basic charges. RS1640 bills peak demand only during 06:00--22:00; RS1650's zero demand fee ends in March 2026 under that historical schedule. We therefore distinguish billed peak from physical grid power and do not extrapolate the temporary concession through 2033.\cite{bctariff2023}

For daily grid energy $E$, available charging hours $T$, site limit $P$, and rate-specific billed hours $T_j$, solve
\begin{equation}
 \min M_j:\quad \sum_{t\in T}p_t\Delta t=E,\quad
 0\le p_t\le P,\quad p_t\le M_j\ (t\in T_j).
\end{equation}
Power is zero outside $T$. If $h_0$ available hours are outside the billing window and $h_1>0$ are inside, the sharp lower bound is $M_j\ge\max\{0,(E-h_0P)/h_1\}$. Physical feasibility also requires $E\le(h_0+h_1)P$; a wholly nonbilled feasible window gives $M_j=0$. The executed CPU LP matches this independent bound in every feasible case. A second stage minimizes physical peak at the optimal billed peak. Monthly bills include energy, basic and demand charges, the prior-winter eleven-period minimum-bill floor, and the 1\% rider refund; taxes and capital are excluded.

We normalize to 100 \emph{distance-equivalent units}, each at the historical 68,000 km/year reference. These are not an observed fleet or a one-for-one replacement count. Historical onboard energy of 1.37--2.0 kWh/km, assumed charging efficiencies of 85/90/95\%, three common return windows and 2.5/3.5/5-MW site limits give 243 scenarios. Annual energy is spread evenly across 365 days; this relaxation does not certify winter energy, battery capacity, charger-port occupancy or actual bus availability. A meter-measured intensity would not be divided by efficiency again.\cite{fleet2020}

\subsection{A solved age-cohort planning benchmark}
For London, an original \emph{fleet slot} denotes one available vehicle position of the same body type. Renewing a slot requires suitable electric capacity; the operational model may require additional vehicles. Let $n_c$ be the observed size and $b_c$ the registration year of cohort $c$, and choose a maximum chassis-life scenario $L$. These caps are assumptions for sensitivity, not asserted TfL rules. A non-electric cohort must renew by $d_c=\min(2033,\max(2024,b_c+L))$. An existing electric cohort enters the renewal model only when $b_c+L\le2033$; the 2013 Routemaster retains its chassis vintage despite its 2022 electric retrofit.\cite{tflaudit}

Let integer $a_{cy}$ allocate original slots to renewal years $2024\le y\le d_c$. We first minimize the required peak annual renewal capacity $M$:
\begin{equation}
 \min M:\qquad \sum_{y\le d_c}a_{cy}=n_c,\quad
 \sum_c a_{cy}\le M,\quad a_{cy}\in\mathbb Z_+.
\end{equation}
With $M$ fixed, a second solve minimizes $\sum_{c,y}(d_c-y)a_{cy}$, avoiding avoidable early renewal. Among ties, a third solve prioritizes earlier non-electric conversion without changing either optimum. This is an executed integer program using the public cohort counts, not an assumed uniform purchase schedule.

An independent certificate follows from $D_t=\sum_{c:d_c\le t}n_c$:
\begin{equation}
 M^\star=\max_{t=2024,\ldots,2033}
 \left\lceil\frac{D_t}{t-2023}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Necessity follows because all $D_t$ slots must fit into $t-2023$ annual capacities. Sufficiency follows by allocating the earliest-deadline cohorts first when every cohort may renew from 2024. Later technical release dates, class-specific production limits and charging construction can increase the requirement; the certificate concerns this cohort submodel.

\section{Sensitivity, limitations and decision boundary}
Reliable range is the dominant operational variable in the executed screen. At 149 km for the 40-ft proxy and 150 km for articulated buses, the weekday timetable requires 2,444 sequential battery requests versus 1,502 participating blocks, a 62.7\% increase. At 250/251 km it requires 1,901 (26.6\%), and at 300 km it requires 1,741 (15.9\%). Even the 350-km sensitivity still creates 1,623 requests because several long duties exceed that distance. A 745-km highway block is the longest observed duty and necessarily needs terminal charging, a vehicle exchange or block redesign. These are screening bounds, not physical fleet counts.

To test whether the named cost score is a fragile point choice, we vary seven documented inputs jointly: the 1,711--1,733 fleet bridge, seven paired range cases, 5--18 assumed existing BEBs, 9/17--11/17 baseline renewal, a 1.5--5.0\% discount rate, the hardware versus project-envelope vehicle basis, and four baseline fuel intensities. The resulting 432,768-case Cartesian grid reproduces the named CAD 4.575-billion, $-96.4\%$ cell exactly. Across the equal-weight assumption grid, median gross PV is CAD 5.26 billion and median economic and environment scores are $-127.2\%$ and $33.4\%$; the joint 5th--95th spans are $-166.9$ to $-89.8\%$ and $-15.1$ to $41.5\%$. Range/request count has the largest rank association with economic score, followed by the project-envelope cost basis. These are scenario quantiles, not sampling confidence intervals or probabilities of real outcomes.

Funding does not alter resource cost. In the generic case, 0/25/50\% eligible grants change the agency present value from CAD 4.58 to 3.64 and 2.71 billion, and the score from $-96.4\%$ to $-56.4\%$ and $-16.4\%$. The class-specific central case gives $-75.0/-42.8/-10.5\%$; even its 50\% grant leaves agency PV CAD 0.283 billion above the CAD 2.693-billion renewal comparator. These grant cases cover incremental conversion capital only; the alternative common-trolley grant calculation holds the comparator unsubsidized and cannot establish parity under symmetric eligibility. The transition therefore needs a stated environmental/policy value or lower bids, not a claim of financial break-even. The conventional O\&M mid-rate is 2.77 CAD/vehicle-km; moving that rate to the hybrid 1.97 or diesel 3.30 value changes the class score to $-115.1\%$ or $-57.6\%$. As a separate regional layer, 2023 Bus Operations implies a CAD 0.62--0.97 billion gross-value-added proxy, CAD 0.62--0.79 billion labour income and 6.0--8.8 thousand FTE; these cover the published Bus Operations boundary, not fixed-route service alone. The provided infrastructure cases imply CAD 0.62--3.86 billion of gross construction-period value added over 2024--31, divided across eight years. It is a transition memo, not an additive annual benefit, and construction spending is not credited again as output.\cite{budget2025,statcan2022io,tc2024comp}

The environmental result is sensitive to the baseline fuel mix, but its sign is not robust: the generic ramp scores 0.7\%, 33.7\%, 38.0\% and 41.5\% for the four fuel cases. Replacing just one 162-bus cohort of 335-kWh batteries adds 3.148 kt, exceeding the low-carbon case's 2.743-kt saving and making that score negative. This is a stress case, not a predicted replacement. The inventory excludes charging/depot construction and end-of-life credits. Its 0.302--1.58 kg CO$_2$e/km baseline range and 19 g/km electric operating rate are sourced, while the vehicle and battery factors are transferred from a Latin American example. Adding infrastructure and a battery replacement to a fully matched boundary would lower the score.

The remaining uncertainties are specific and material: a complete January--August 2023 timetable or as-operated records; block-to-vehicle and block-to-garage links; vehicle ages and payload; depot parking, charger ports, firm power and commissioning dates; terminal exchange times; trolley feeder capacity; and 2033 vehicle winter tests. Without them, no city-wide Pareto point can be certified. The class cost also omits battery replacements, retained pre-2024 asset terminal values, resale receipts and infrastructure terminal value; these must be added before calling it a complete total cost of ownership. The current result is a conditional inventory and cost case with an executed feasibility screen, not a claim that every 2033 trip is schedulable.

The three numerical implementation gates are: every scheduled trip is retained; every candidate block satisfies class, capacity, transfer, winter SOC and charger/site checks; and every cost and emission term has a source, year, unit and uncertainty range. Code currently passes the first structurally and returns \texttt{UNVERIFIED} when the second or third lacks inputs.

\subsection{Conclusion}
The model supports an accelerated class-specific pathway but not an unconditional 2033 procurement order. An assumed 1,647-route-BEB conversion plus 262 common trolley renewals zeros conventional stock arithmetically; service preservation is unproved, and 18 initial BEBs are assumed. Its central price-mixed economics are CAD 4.71 billion versus CAD 2.69 billion for ordinary renewal plus operation, and its partial-production environmental reduction is 4.6--42.7\%. Double-deck and shuttle duties require certified terminal or en-route charging before those purchase counts become service-feasible. The defensible near-term decision is to begin grid and depot work immediately, procure proven heavy buses first, and make each later cohort contingent on published charger, SOC, capacity, winter-test and cost certificates.

\section*{Letter to a Metro Vancouver decision-maker}
\textbf{To:} TransLink Board and Mayors' Council\\
\textbf{Subject:} Conditions for a service-preserving all-electric fixed-route bus fleet by 2033

Dear decision-makers,

We treat 2033 as an accelerated target against TransLink's published 2040 goal. The recovered fall-2023 GTFS schedules 54,497 fixed-route bus trips across 25 service patterns; the representative weekday has 1,752 source-declared duty blocks and 1,087 simultaneous trips. We would retain every departure, route direction and capacity proxy in that timetable. Existing trolleybuses remain part of the electric baseline.

Under a 300-km overnight-charge sensitivity, the weekday timetable creates 1,741 sequential battery-block requests for 1,502 participating blocks, 15.9\% more duty requests. A 700-kWh double-decker still leaves 17 of 57 duties short of one overnight charge, and a 150-kWh shuttle leaves 49 of 101. These are screening positions, not purchased bus counts: garage links, terminal exchange times, payload and site power are not public.

In the conditional inventory scenario, 1,647 route BEBs replace 1,445 conventional buses and add 202 duty positions; 262 further electric purchases renew the already-electric trolley fleet one-for-one. Gross present value is CAD 4.71 billion against CAD 2.69 billion, an economic score of $-75.0\%$. A 25\% grant gives $-42.8\%$; a 50\% grant gives $-10.5\%$. The gross resource cost is unchanged, and the high class envelope reaches CAD 5.26 billion ($-95.5\%$). Its 1,927-electric-asset endpoint is stock arithmetic, not a service-feasibility certificate.

The class-specific partial-production, operating and maintenance boundary yields a 4.6--42.7\% emissions reduction. The 2023 fixed-route tailpipe proxy is 126--408 tonnes NO$_x$, 1.11--8.34 tonnes PM$_{2.5}$ and 2.34--14.18 tonnes PM$_{10}$ per year. Depot construction and end-of-life credits remain excluded. The published Bus Operations umbrella is also associated with CAD 0.62--0.97 billion of annual regional value-added and 6.0--8.8 thousand FTE; construction-period effects are separate and non-additive.

Start depot design, utility interconnection and permits in 2024; the published delivery range is six to eight years. Determine purchase quantities through dispatch and commissioned-site checks, test 40- and 60-foot buses under fully electric winter conditions, and hold double-deck and shuttle conversion behind in-route charging and verified payload. If a block fails time, SOC, charger or site capacity, publish that exact gap rather than deleting service.

Sincerely,\\
Fleet Transition Study Team
