{\Large\bfseries Electrifying a Metro Bus Fleet Under a Preserved Service Floor}
\end{center}

\textbf{Question and baseline.} We study a conditional conversion of TransLink's fixed-route bus service to zero-tailpipe electric propulsion and all-electric accessories by 2033, subject to retaining recovered fall-2023 departures and capacity proxies. Existing trolleybuses count as electric; ordinary renewal is the comparator. Electricity-source compliance is treated separately.

\textbf{Model.} We state the full master model, then execute bounded submodels: trip and SOC validation, a route-level duty sample, a proportional city inventory, and age- or capacity-constrained cohort models. Service cannot be converted before a facility-readiness wave. Gross and grant-adjusted costs use constant 2023 CAD; emissions use the same annual service ledger and explicit partial boundaries. A city-wide Pareto frontier remains unavailable.

\textbf{Observed result.} The recovered fall-2023 GTFS contains 54,497 trips in 25 service patterns. A full-pattern absolute-time screen retains all 495,198 trip instances; maximum exact-class concurrency is 811 heavy, 107 shuttle, 37 double-deck and 147 trolley positions. The representative weekday has 1,752 source blocks and 1,087 simultaneous trips; the maximum trip day has 22,494 trips. Traction-only winter demand reaches 469 MWh/day, or 65.2 MW averaged over eight hours before accessories. Annual fixed-route service is 5.58 million hours and 106.87 million km. Vehicle identity, depot, chargers and firm power remain unresolved.\cite{gtfs2023,budget2025,eea2023,prati2025,bcvanderhoof}

\textbf{Conditional inventory, cost and environmental scenarios.} At 300 km, the representative weekday yields 1,698--1,741 distance requests; all 25 recovered patterns reach at most 1,745. An assumed class inventory buys 1,647 route vehicles and 262 common trolley renewals (1,909 electric purchases), ending at 1,927 electric and zero conventional stock. Central constant-price PV is CAD 4.43 billion versus CAD 2.64 billion ($-67.7\%$); symmetric 0/25/50\% grants give $-67.7/-38.0/-5.0\%$. Adding omitted charger, battery and operator lifecycle reserves gives $-68.7$ to $-71.1\%$; retained-asset and infrastructure terminal-value cases span $-75.1$ to $-20.6\%$. Adding depot and charger embodied carbon to the scored-conversion horizon gives environmental scores $-67.0$ to $3.4\%$ across renewal fuels, versus a partial-boundary range of $-14.1$ to $16.3\%$. These are inventory scenarios, not dispatch certificates. London's 17-year renewal peak is 776 slots per year.\cite{fleet2020,playbook2024,budget2025,foothill2024,endera2023,zhao2022,yeboah2025}

\textbf{Recommendation.} Start depot power and grid work in 2024, publish each site's release date, and procure against commissioned capacity. Validate a terminal-level duty plan before treating block ratios as fleet counts. Gate double-deck and shuttle conversion on in-route charging, winter tests and verified payload.

\textbf{Keywords:} electric bus; fleet replacement; service preservation; charging; life-cycle cost.

\section{Problem definition and study boundary}
The competition asks for environmental and financial consequences, a ten-year transition in three metropolitan areas, and a decision-maker letter.\cite{problem} We take end-2023 as Vancouver's decision baseline and plan 2024--2033. Observed fall schedules and annual service totals define the service evidence; 2022 and 2025 fleet snapshots only seed named stock scenarios. No later purchase is treated as a known 2023 decision. Metro Vancouver had 2,642,825 residents in 2021, above the problem threshold.\cite{vancouvercensus} Chicago and London use their separately dated 2023 registers; London's March audit is not treated as December stock.

Prior fleet models optimize electric vehicle count and mix jointly with charging and scheduling,\cite{rogge} use a multi-period purchase, salvage and demand-charge formulation,\cite{pelletier} or solve multi-depot time-window and recharge constraints.\cite{gkiotsalitis} This study connects those ideas to a recovered public timetable, a capacity proxy where the vehicle roster is absent, a normal-renewal comparator, and a separately stated emissions boundary. The result is also a data-validity test: a missing garage or winter record changes the status to \texttt{UNVERIFIED} rather than becoming a zero-cost assumption.

We adopt an operational endpoint: electric propulsion, all-electric accessories and zero tailpipe combustion. The problem's phrase ``zero-emission electric source'' can also require a verified supply chain. No contract, hourly matching or certificate is in evidence, so the strict-source endpoint remains \texttt{UNVERIFIED}; the environment model includes an average-grid attribution and a zero-source contract sensitivity. A diesel heater, renewable diesel, RNG or hybrid does not satisfy the operational endpoint. Hydrogen remains outside the electric count until a zero-emission source is certified. Existing trolleys satisfy the drivetrain condition, subject to feeder feasibility. TransLink's 2040 target makes 2033 an accelerated counterfactual.\cite{fleet2020,playbook2024}

BC Hydro's service plan records a 100\% Clean Electricity Standard over a fixed January 2021--December 2024 portfolio, based on its own clean generation, clean contracts and net Powerex deliveries; its 2023/24 forecast still includes 4,167 GWh of system imports and 312 GWh of thermal and other supply. The provincial 2023 integrated-grid factor is 11.3 t CO$_2$e/GWh, not zero. A clean annual portfolio therefore does not certify hourly zero-source energy behind each charger, and no TransLink purchase contract or certificate is in the evidence set. We retain the nonzero average factor in central scoring and use zero only as an explicitly unverified contract sensitivity.\cite{bchydro2023service,bcgrid}

The decision unit is year $y$, class $k$, depot $d$, trip $i$, vehicle $v$ and charger interval $t$. Classes include standard, articulated, highway coach, double-deck, community shuttle and trolley. The service target is the recovered fall timetable; January--August 2023 departures are not available. We do not reduce that recovered service to improve feasibility.

Its first-stop distance field is absent for every trip, so exact trip length is unknown; a monotonic shape-distance envelope gives a median interval width of 0.432 km, about 4.3\%, and a maximum 26.309 km. Route and arrival data are used by permission of TransLink, which assumes no responsibility for their accuracy or currency.\cite{gtfs2023}

The 2018 official service guidelines define peak load factor as average peak passengers divided by practical vehicle capacity. Their reference capacities are 24 (mini-bus), 47 (standard trolley), 50 (standard bus or coach), 70 (articulated trolley), and 75 (articulated bus). Dividing each positive 2023 time-bin load by its load factor reproduces one of these values in 8,705 of 9,371 cells; a further 344 cells give exactly 85, consistent with TransLink's separately described 80--86-seat double-deck vehicles. Thus 9,049 cells (96.56\%) match six capacity clusters, and 322 do not. Across 180 routes with positive cells, 34 show more than one cluster or an unmatched cell. This is a useful \emph{aggregate vehicle-class diagnostic}, not a trip-level vehicle assignment: capacities are historical averages, the 85-person class is inferred, and mixed or differently averaged cells cannot establish an individual bus's capacity. The reproducible cell-level calculation and every unmatched value are retained in the data supplement.\cite{guidelines2018,doubledeck2017,loadcsv}

Capacity must also be checked on the replacement vehicle. Current TransLink operating limits are 20 passengers for community shuttles, 77 for 40-ft buses and 120 for articulated buses; the 2018 planning values are lower and distinguish seating from practical capacity.\cite{events2026,guidelines2018} Manufacturer examples span 68 passengers for a Nova LFSe+, 88 for a current 40-ft Xcelsior CHARGE NG, 118 for the 2023 XE60 specification and 123 for the current XE60. A smaller electric bus therefore requires another departure vehicle or a different model when the baseline departure has 77 or 120 spaces.\cite{nova2025,newflyer2025}

The 2023 accountability report also records 112,591 tonnes CO$_2$e of \emph{revenue-fleet operating} emissions, comprising 110,402 Scope-1 and 2,189 Scope-2 tonnes; it separately reports 33,718 tonnes of biogenic CO$_2$ from renewable fuels. The revenue-fleet number covers bus and non-bus modes, excludes vehicle and station manufacture, and reflects that year's renewable-fuel mix (96\% of purchased bus natural gas was renewable). It is therefore an independent accounting check and an upper bound on the included bus operating inventory, not a substitute for the 2024--2033 same-service, life-cycle normal-renewal baseline.\cite{accountability}

A later 18 November 2025 CMBC allocation table provides a retrospective fleet-profile check: 1,733 active buses at seven sites, including 261 trolleys, 22 battery buses, 535 hybrids, 365 CNG, 379 diesels and 171 gasoline shuttles; 63 more are in storage. The 2020 TransLink fleet transition plan records 1,672 CMBC vehicles, including 188 standard and 74 articulated trolleybuses.\cite{fleet2020} Same-day December evidence gives an exact 280 electric assets and an approximate split of 275 trolleys plus five BEBs; this becomes the central split. TransLink's prototype travelled more than 150 km on one charge in winter and added a useful overhead charge in less than six minutes. \section{Fleet and service model}
\subsection{Decision variables and normal-renewal baseline}
Let $x^p_{ykd}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ be new buses of propulsion $p$ delivered to class $k$ and depot $d$; $r^p_{ykda}$ vehicles retired from age cohort $a$; $h_{ydq}$ charger positions; and $z_{iv}\in\{0,1\}$ a trip assignment. Let $N^p_{ykda}$ be stock and $p\in\{\mathrm{conventional},\mathrm{battery},\mathrm{trolley}\}$ denote disjoint propulsion categories. Total purchases are $x_{ykd}=\sum_p x^p_{ykd}$. Historical expenditure is sunk; early retirement enters through residual value or sale receipts.

For each class and depot, without used-vehicle purchases or inter-depot transfers,
\begin{equation}
 N^p_{y,k,d,0}=x^p_{y,k,d},\qquad
 N^p_{y,k,d,a}=N^p_{y-1,k,d,a-1}-r^p_{y,k,d,a}\quad(a\ge1),
\end{equation}
with $0\le r^p_{y,k,d,a}\le N^p_{y-1,k,d,a-1}$ and integer stocks. Transfers or used purchases would add explicit inflow and outflow terms. No diesel, hybrid, CNG or hydrogen propulsion or non-electric accessory may operate on a fixed-route trip in 2033. Purchases are not one-for-one replacements: schedule, range, spare and charging evidence would determine the physical count.

\subsection{Hard service and technical constraints}
For every recovered fall service pattern $\omega$, every trip $i\in I_\omega$ is retained at its route, direction and departure time. Multiple smaller buses may serve one departure when each is compatible and their passenger allocations do not interact:
\begin{align}
 \sum_{v\in V_i}z_{iv}&\geq 1 &&(i\in I),\\
 \sum_{v\in V_i}Q_vz_{iv}&\geq Q_i^0 &&(i\in I).
\end{align}
Here $Q_i^0$ is a capacity proxy, not observed passenger demand. The route--direction peak is used only as an aggregate diagnostic because a time-bin maximum need not coincide with a particular trip set. Accessible trips require accessible vehicles; spare stock and class compatibility remain constraints.

The source block is a duty hypothesis. For consecutive trips $i\prec j$ on one vehicle, $s_j-e_i\ge\tau^{road}_{ij}+t^{turn}_{ij}$. Under a uniform five-minute turn and 50 km/h straight-line transfer, 4,588 of 20,679 weekday connections fail $\Delta t\ge5+60d_{ij}/50$, including short same-stop gaps. This is an assumed turn policy, not observed operational failure. All 200 Route 100 connections pass: 223 trips and 23 source blocks become 25 fragments under 300 km, with a simultaneous-trip lower bound of 14. Garage, charger and road-time certificates remain absent.

The maximum trip day has 22,494 trips; 8 September has the maximum block count and 22,492 trips. The annual output also reproduces 5,575,772 hours, 106,868,324 service km and 5,715,791,401 capacity km within documented measurement differences. These calibrations cannot substitute for departure, block and site constraints.\cite{gtfs2023,budget2025}

The executed range screen uses
\begin{equation}
d_b^L=\sum_{i\in b}\ell_i^L,\qquad d_b^U=\sum_{i\in b}\ell_i^U,\qquad
n_{bk}^{L,(j)}=\left\lceil\frac{d_b^L}{R_k^{(j)}}\right\rceil,\qquad
n_{bk}^{U,(j)}=\left\lceil\frac{d_b^U}{R_k^{(j)}}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
For the representative weekday and $R=300$ km, the two sums are 1,698 and 1,741. Across all 25 recovered patterns, upper-distance requests range from 762 to 1,745. These are fixed-range, no-mid-duty-recharge distance screens. Deadhead, terminal charging and task reuse can move the physical requirement in either direction, so neither endpoint is a vehicle lower bound.

\begin{samepage}
Each vehicle's assigned trips and charging intervals cannot overlap. Location-to-location deadhead times must fit between events, including departure from and return to its depot. Driving to or from service also removes a vehicle from the available depot spare count. For battery vehicles, energy follows
\begin{align}
 e_{v,t+1}&=e_{vt}+\eta_v c_{vt}
   -\epsilon_{vk}^{\rm traction,winter}(\textstyle\sum_i z_{iv}\ell_{it}+\ell^{\rm deadhead}_{vt})
   -E^{\rm accessories}_{vt},\\
 \underline{s}_vB_{v,y}&\le e_{vt}\le \bar{s}_vB_{v,y},\\
 c_{vt}&=\textstyle\sum_d P_{vdt}\Delta t,\qquad
 e_{v,n,T}=e_{v,n+1,0},\qquad e_{v,N,T}\ge e_{v,1,0}.
\end{align}
\end{samepage}
Here $\ell_{it}$ is trip $i$'s interval distance, $c_{vt}$ grid energy and $B_{v,y}$ degraded capacity. Days $n=1,\ldots,N$ have $T\Delta t=1440$ minutes; $u_{vdt}\in\{0,1\}$. The validator uses $1440(n-1)+t$, preserving GTFS times after 24:00. Two cycle copies expose wrap conflicts; SOC closes at the same vehicle-event phase. Midnight never resets location or energy; auxiliaries run through layovers. Missing winter loads yield \texttt{UNVERIFIED}. Site constraints are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 0&\le P_{vdt}\le\bar P_{vd}u_{vdt},\qquad \sum_d u_{vdt}\le1,\\
 \sum_v u_{vdt}&\le H_{dt},\qquad \sum_v P_{vdt}\le P^{\rm grid}_{dt},\qquad
 H_{dt}=0\ \mathrm{until\ commissioned}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Conventional baseline vehicles instead require a fuel budget, with jointly calibrated distance and elapsed-duty rates $a_v,b_v$ in a consistent fuel unit (L or kg):
\begin{equation}
 F_{v,t+1}=F_{vt}-a_v\ell_{vt}-b_v\Delta t,\qquad
 F_v^{\rm reserve}\le F_{vt}\le F_v^{\rm tank}.
\end{equation}
The implemented check assumes no refuelling within a duty; the time rate includes stationary consumption and must not duplicate the distance rate. Missing fuel data leave the normal-renewal comparator unverified. Such vehicles may serve intermediate years but cannot satisfy the strict 2033 endpoint.

Trolleybuses use a separate wired-network check. Each trip, deadhead and powered layover needs a certified connected path and an operating feeder. Concurrent demand must obey $\sum_{v\in V_{ft}}P_v\le P_f^{\rm firm}$, where firm power is available after other loads and reserve. The code conservatively reserves each vehicle's measured all-in peak draw on every feeder traversed for the entire moving segment; stationary accessories use their separate demand. A failure of this sufficient power certificate calls for finer feeder timing before declaring physical infeasibility. Missing path or feeder records leave existing trolley operation unverified, without assigning these vehicles fictitious batteries or buying their capacity again.

Depot bus parking capacity, charger footprint, charging efficiency, construction lead time and route-specific winter testing must also pass. A charger site may be the home depot or a route terminal, provided the location and travel-time checks pass. The accompanying validator checks the supplied trip, energy and site records, returning \texttt{UNVERIFIED} for missing essential inputs; it cannot certify a city-wide solution from time-bin averages.

\subsection{Explicit assumptions and validation}
Five assumptions define the evidence boundary. (A1) Every recovered fall departure and direction is a service obligation; the representative weekday is a planning case. (A2) The values 24, 47, 50, 70, 75 and 85 are capacity proxies. (A3) Reliable range is a technology scenario.  (A4) The 1,725-bus stock and approximately five existing BEBs are scenario parameters, with the stock and retained-BEB grids varied jointly. (A5) Price envelopes are rebased to 2023 CAD using Bank of Canada CPI; CPI is not a construction index, so the 3\% rate is a real-rate scenario. Regression tests reject depletion on day two, next-day charging that overlaps driving or site use, and insufficient cross-midnight travel time; valid overnight service passes. The local pipeline does not certify a city-wide schedule.

\subsection{Optimization and executed decomposition}
The complete master problem would link purchases and age cohorts to available vehicles, trip assignments, daily SOC, site commissioning and the renewal comparator:
\begin{equation}
\min C^{\rm gross}\quad\text{s.t. }
\begin{aligned}
&\text{service, block and capacity;}\\
&\text{SOC, charger and feeder;}\\
&\text{spare, inventory, commissioning and endpoint.}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This is a design statement, not a solved city MILP. The exact vehicle, site and feeder records are incomplete, so no city Pareto frontier is claimed. We execute six bounded components: all-pattern absolute-time and block screens; a Route 100 terminal sample; proportional inventory cost scenarios; cost and infrastructure-emission bridges; capacity-constrained Chicago cohorts; and body-constrained London cohorts. The validator returns \texttt{UNVERIFIED} when an event or site record is absent.\cite{rogge,pelletier,gkiotsalitis}

\section{Costs, funding and environmental accounting}
Let $K_y$ include buses, initial batteries, chargers, depot civil work, grid upgrades, design and training. Let $O_y$ include energy, maintenance, replacement batteries, drivers, demand charges and site upkeep. Let $S_y$ be realized sale receipts and $R_{2033}$ all vehicles and durable infrastructure remaining at the horizon. Public envelopes are rebased with the Bank of Canada total CPI to 2023 CAD; the 3\% rate is then a real-rate scenario.\cite{cpi2025} CPI is economy-wide, so construction costs remain a material source of uncertainty.
\begin{align}
 C^{\rm gross}
 &=\sum_{y=2024}^{2033}\frac{K_y+O_y-S_y}{(1+r)^{y-2023}}
 -\frac{R_{2033}}{(1+r)^{10}},\\
 \Delta C&=C^{\rm gross}_{\rm electric}-C^{\rm gross}_{\rm renewal}.
\end{align}
For grant rate $f\in\{0,0.25,0.50\}$ and eligible expenditures $E_y$, $C^{\rm agency}=C^{\rm gross}-\sum_y fE_y/(1+r)^{y-2023}$. A grant changes who pays, not gross buses, copper, electricity or labour. Local employment and procurement impacts require an additional regional economic model and are reported separately to avoid double counting.\cite{usdot}

The regional layer records direct local procurement \(L_y\), wages \(W_y\) and job-years \(J_y\). Any induced value added uses a regional input--output multiplier \(m_y\) on labour and supplier value added, \(V_y^{\rm local}=m_y(L_y+W_y)\), not on the entire gross construction outlay. Feasible charging capacity, reliability and terminal asset value enter the fleet model through served trips, avoided extra vehicles, bill savings and residual value. Construction spending is not counted again as a benefit.

Define a life-cycle greenhouse-gas inventory over equal service:
\begin{align}
 G={}&G_{\rm vehicle+battery}+G_{\rm infrastructure}\\
 &+\sum_y\left(G_{\rm fuel,y}+G_{\rm electricity,y}
 +G_{\rm maintenance,y}\right)-G_{\rm end\ of\ life}.
\end{align}
The executed inventory includes vehicle and battery production, operating energy and maintenance. It excludes depot construction and end-of-life credits. British Columbia's 2023 integrated-grid factor, 0.0113 kg CO$_2$e/kWh, is used as an annual average; a zero-source contract sensitivity sets electric operating emissions to zero without claiming that a certificate exists.\cite{bcgrid} NO$_x$, particles, electricity and battery production remain separate. The denominator covers scored conversion service only because common trolley and retained-BEB terms are not measured on both sides.

The requested scores are
\begin{equation}
 S_C=100\frac{C_{\rm renewal}-C_{\rm electric}}{C_{\rm renewal}},\qquad
 S_G=100\frac{G_{\rm renewal}-G_{\rm electric}}{G_{\rm renewal}}.
\end{equation}
Negative values remain negative. The economic score is based on \emph{gross} cost, with subsidized agency cost shown beside it; otherwise the 50\% grant would artificially change the societal merit ranking.

\subsection{Charging tariffs and the value of firm power}
The original 2023 budget assumes diesel at CAD 1.66/L; this is a planning price, not an actual invoice. Its 32.17-million-L operational volume includes a broader system boundary and is not treated as fixed-route bus consumption.\cite{budget2023} BC Hydro's April-2023 tariff supplies energy, monthly demand and daily basic charges. RS1640 bills peak demand only during 06:00--22:00; RS1650's zero demand fee ends in March 2026 under that historical schedule. We therefore distinguish billed peak from physical grid power and do not extrapolate the temporary concession through 2033.\cite{bctariff2023}

For daily grid energy $E$, available charging hours $T$, site limit $P$, and rate-specific billed hours $T_j$, solve
\begin{equation}
 \min M_j:\quad \sum_{t\in T}p_t\Delta t=E,\quad
 0\le p_t\le P,\quad p_t\le M_j\ (t\in T_j).
\end{equation}
Power is zero outside $T$. If $h_0$ available hours are outside the billing window and $h_1>0$ are inside, the sharp lower bound is $M_j\ge\max\{0,(E-h_0P)/h_1\}$. Physical feasibility also requires $E\le(h_0+h_1)P$; a wholly nonbilled feasible window gives $M_j=0$. The executed CPU LP matches this independent bound in every feasible case. A second stage minimizes physical peak at the optimal billed peak. Monthly bills include energy, basic and demand charges, the prior-winter eleven-period minimum-bill floor, and the 1\% rider refund; taxes and capital are excluded.

We normalize to 100 \emph{distance-equivalent units}, each at the historical 68,000 km/year reference. These are not an observed fleet or a one-for-one replacement count. Historical onboard energy of 1.37--2.0 kWh/km, assumed charging efficiencies of 85/90/95\%, three common return windows and 2.5/3.5/5-MW site limits give 243 scenarios. Annual energy is spread evenly across 365 days; this relaxation does not certify winter energy, battery capacity, charger-port occupancy or actual bus availability. A meter-measured intensity would not be divided by efficiency again.\cite{fleet2020}

\subsection{A solved age-cohort planning benchmark}
For London, an original \emph{fleet slot} denotes one available vehicle position of the same body type. Renewing a slot requires suitable electric capacity; the operational model may require additional vehicles. Let $n_c$ be the observed size and $b_c$ the registration year of cohort $c$, and choose a maximum chassis-life scenario $L$. These caps are assumptions for sensitivity, not asserted TfL rules. A non-electric cohort must renew by $d_c=\min(2033,\max(2024,b_c+L))$. An existing electric cohort enters the renewal model only when $b_c+L\le2033$; the 2013 Routemaster retains its chassis vintage despite its 2022 electric retrofit.\cite{tflaudit}

Let integer $a_{cy}$ allocate original slots to renewal years $2024\le y\le d_c$. We first minimize the required peak annual renewal capacity $M$:
\begin{equation}
 \min M:\qquad \sum_{y\le d_c}a_{cy}=n_c,\quad
 \sum_c a_{cy}\le M,\quad 0\le a_{cy}\le n_c,\quad a_{cy}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\end{equation}
With $M$ fixed, a second solve minimizes $\sum_{c,y}(d_c-y)a_{cy}$, avoiding avoidable early renewal. Among ties, a third solve prioritizes earlier non-electric conversion without changing either optimum. This is an executed integer program using the public cohort counts, not an assumed uniform purchase schedule.

An independent certificate follows from $D_t=\sum_{c:d_c\le t}n_c$:
\begin{equation}
 M^\star=\max_{t=2024,\ldots,2033}
 \left\lceil\frac{D_t}{t-2023}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Necessity follows because all $D_t$ slots must fit into $t-2023$ annual capacities. Sufficiency follows by allocating the earliest-deadline cohorts first when every cohort may renew from 2024. Later technical release dates, class-specific production limits and charging construction can increase the requirement; the certificate concerns this cohort submodel.

\section{Sensitivity, limitations and decision boundary}
Reliable range is the dominant operational variable. At 149/150 km the weekday requires 2,444 sequential requests versus 1,502 participating blocks; at 300 km it requires 1,698--1,741, and at 350 km 1,587--1,623 because long duties remain. All 25 patterns at 300 km reach an upper-distance maximum of 1,745. A 745-km highway block requires terminal charging or exchange. These are distance screens; task timing and vehicle reuse can move the count in both directions.

The 432,768-cell grid varies the 1,711--1,733 stock scenario, seven paired ranges, 5--18 retained BEBs, 9/17--11/17 renewal, a 1.5--5.0\% rate, vehicle cost basis and four fuel intensities. The annual service kilometre denominator is held fixed; fleet variation changes capital and position counts alone. The named cell reproduces CAD 4.318 billion and $-88.2\%$. Median gross PV is CAD 4.77 billion; economic median and 5th--95th are $-112.0\%$ and $-137.8$ to $-81.9\%$; environment median and 5th--95th are $8.1\%$ and $-32.9$ to $15.3\%$. These are equal-weight assumptions, not confidence intervals.

Funding does not alter resource cost. In the generic case, 0/25/50\% grants change agency PV from CAD 4.32 to 3.42 and 2.52 billion, and the score from $-88.2$ to $-49.0$ and $-9.7\%$. The class central case gives $-67.7/-38.0/-5.0\%$. Common trolley renewal receives the same percentage in both comparator and plan; the former one-sided grant case is retained only as an explicit policy counterfactual. The transition still needs policy value or lower bids. The conventional O\&M rate sensitivity remains conditional. The regional memo gives CAD 0.62--0.97 billion annual gross-value-added proxy and 6.0--8.8 thousand FTE for the wider Bus Operations boundary; construction-period output is separate and non-additive.\cite{budget2025,statcan2022io,tc2024comp}

The partial environmental sign is not robust. Generic scores are $-17.3\%$, $9.1\%$, $12.5\%$ and $15.2\%$; class scores are $-14.1\%$, $10.6\%$, $13.7\%$ and $16.3\%$. A stress replacement of the 322-bus first delivery cohort adds 6.256 kt and moves the generic renewable-fuel case to $-18.9\%$. This is not a predicted replacement year. The 0.302--1.58 kg CO$_2$e/km baseline and 19 g/km electric rates remain transfer-sensitive. The separate infrastructure bridge reverses the partial sign except for fossil-diesel renewal; road-vehicle end-of-life remains outside the 2033 horizon.

The remaining uncertainties are a January--August timetable or as-operated records; block-to-vehicle and block-to-garage links; vehicle ages and payload; depot parking, ports, firm power and commissioning dates; terminal exchange times; trolley feeder capacity; and 2033 winter tests. Asset and infrastructure terminal values are now bracketed, but uniform initial ages and resale shares remain scenarios; future battery replacement lies beyond this purchase schedule and is not capitalized. No city-wide Pareto point or full TCO is certified.

The implementation gates are: every recovered scheduled trip is retained; every candidate day satisfies class, capacity, road transfer, winter SOC, charger/site and periodic closure; and every cost and emission term has a source, year, unit and uncertainty range. The validator returns \texttt{UNVERIFIED} when the second or third lacks inputs.

\subsection{Conclusion}
The 1,647 route purchases and 262 trolley renewals remain conditional; service feasibility is unproved. Central PV is CAD 4.43 versus 2.64 billion ($-67.7\%$); operational-cost bridge and asset/infrastructure cases span $-68.7$ to $-71.1\%$ and $-75.1$ to $-20.6\%$. Infrastructure-inclusive environmental scores span $-67.0$ to $3.4\%$. Gate procurement on site, winter SOC and bid evidence.

\section*{Letter to a Metro Vancouver decision-maker}
\textbf{To:} TransLink Board and Mayors' Council\\
\textbf{Subject:} Conditions for a zero-tailpipe fixed-route bus fleet by 2033

Dear decision-makers,

We treat 2033 as an accelerated target against TransLink's 2040 goal. The recovered fall-2023 GTFS schedules 54,497 fixed-route trips across 25 patterns; a full-pattern screen retains all 495,198 trip instances, with maximum exact-class concurrency of 811 heavy, 107 shuttle, 37 double-deck and 147 trolley positions. Existing trolleybuses remain in the electric baseline. Strict clean-electricity sourcing is unverified.

At 300 km, the weekday yields 1,698--1,741 distance requests for 1,502 participating blocks. The worst pattern has 265 over-range blocks, and the declared five-minute turnover policy rejects 4,621 of 20,736 source connections. Traction-only winter demand reaches 469 MWh/day, or 65.2 MW averaged over eight hours before accessories. A 700-kWh double-decker leaves 17 of 57 duties short of one overnight charge; a 150-kWh shuttle leaves 49 of 101. Nominal depot spaces exist, but site occupancy, firm power, chargers and payload approval remain absent.

The assumed class inventory buys 1,647 route vehicles and 262 common trolley renewals. Constant 2023 gross PV is CAD 4.43 billion against CAD 2.64 billion ($-67.7\%$); missing operational-cost reserves move that to $-68.7$ to $-71.1\%$. With common trolley renewal treated symmetrically, 25/50\% grants give $-38.0/-5.0\%$. Retained-asset and infrastructure terminal-value cases span $-75.1$ to $-20.6\%$. The 1,927-electric-asset endpoint is stock arithmetic, not a dispatch certificate.

The partial production/operation/maintenance boundary yields $-14.1$ to $16.3\%$ in the class case. Adding depot and charger embodied carbon gives $-67.0$ to $3.4\%$; only fossil-diesel renewal retains a positive score. The 2023 tailpipe proxy is 126--408 t NO$_x$, 1.11--8.34 t PM$_{2.5}$ and 2.34--14.18 t PM$_{10}$ per year. The Bus Operations umbrella is associated with CAD 0.62--0.97 billion annual value-added and 6.0--8.8 thousand FTE; construction effects are separate.

Start depot design, interconnection and permits in 2024; no conversion is credited before 2030. Determine quantities through dispatch and commissioned-site checks, test 40- and 60-foot buses under all-electric winter conditions, and hold double-deck and shuttle conversion behind terminal charging and payload certification. If a duty fails time, SOC or site capacity, publish that gap rather than deleting service.

Sincerely,\\
Fleet Transition Study Team
