# Regional economic contribution and bus tailpipe NOx/PM audit

Status on 18 October 2026: executed, source-audited, and suitable as a
supplementary quantitative layer. The air-quality result remains a fuel-allocation
proxy because no 2023 vehicle-kilometre record split by propulsion and fuel was
available. No 2023 HiMCM solution was consulted.

The canonical machine-readable result is
`results/regional_and_air_quality_audit.json`. The tables below are generated by
`src/audit_regional_and_air_quality.py`; rerunning that script recreates the two
processed CSV/JSON pairs.

## A. Regional economic boundary

### A.1 Accounting rule

There are three separate objects, and they must not be added into one
"benefit" number:

1. **Recurring operations:** the 2023 published Bus Operations budget and its
   payroll/supply-chain effects.
2. **Transitional infrastructure:** a separate one-time gross value-added
   envelope over the 2024--2031 build phase.
3. **Vehicle manufacturing:** a memo ceiling only, excluded from the core
   result because the evidence does not establish final bus assembly in BC.

Capex is not revenue and is not a return. In the capital calculation, the
spending is an input to a gross value-added envelope (domestic labour,
construction margin and local supply chain). That envelope is never added to
the recurring operating impact or presented as a net societal benefit. Grants
and tax transfers also remain transfers; they are not extra regional output.

The published `Bus Operations` account is broader than fixed-route bus service:
it includes SeaBus and Access Transit/HandyDART. For that reason the economic
table is labelled as a **Bus Operations umbrella**, while the air-quality table
is fixed-route bus only. The fixed-route operating-cost split cannot be inferred
from the available expense categories.

retention evidence. The retained non-labour supplier amount is
`q(E-W) = CAD 236.598 million/year`; it is not added again as a benefit.

### A.3 Low/Central/High formula

Statistics Canada Table 36-10-0595-01 (2022, British Columbia, within-province)
was used for sector proxies. The direct/simple/total values for the selected
row `Other transit and ground passenger transportation and scenic and
sightseeing transportation [BS48A000]` are:

| Multiplier | GDP per CAD output | Jobs per CAD million output | Labour income per CAD output |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Direct | 0.619 | 4.891 | 0.429 |
| Simple (direct + indirect) | 0.806 | 6.174 | 0.509 |
| Total (direct + indirect + induced) | 1.097 | 8.027 | 0.595 |

The row is used as a **sector proxy**, not as an identified TransLink multiplier.
The directly published `Urban transit systems [BS485100]` row was rejected as a
base multiplier: its direct labour-income coefficient is 1.344 per dollar of
output while its direct GDP coefficient is only 0.098. Those values are not
internally usable for a benefit calculation under public-transit transfer
accounting.

Let `D = W / 102,872 = 6,019.1` direct FTE. This is a payroll-derived estimate
using the Canada-wide urban-transit compensation benchmark because a matching
BC-only 2023 compensation row was not available. Normalize the sector's simple and
total multipliers by its direct multiplier:

`bG_s = 0.806/0.619 = 1.3021`, `bG_t = 1.097/0.619 = 1.7722`,

`bJ_s = 6.174/4.891 = 1.2623`, `bJ_t = 8.027/4.891 = 1.6412`,

`bY_s = 0.509/0.429 = 1.1865`, `bY_t = 0.595/0.429 = 1.3869`.

The scenarios are then a labour-anchored envelope:

`G_L = W` (measured payroll floor, not completed GDP);

`G_C = W [1 + q(bG_s - 1)]`;

`G_H = W [1 + q(bG_t - 1)]`;

`J_C = D [1 + q(bJ_s - 1)]`, `J_H = D [1 + q(bJ_t - 1)]`;

`Y_C = W [1 + q(bY_s - 1)]`, `Y_H = W [1 + q(bY_t - 1)]`.

`q` is applied only to the spillover above the measured direct payroll. This
keeps non-BC suppliers from being awarded as local value added and avoids
counting the same wage base once as a direct effect and again as a multiplier.
The High case includes induced household effects and is therefore a gross
support measure, not a net benefit or an agency revenue result.

## B. Bus-only tailpipe NOx and PM

### B.1 Boundary and activity allocation

The result is **fixed-route bus tailpipe exhaust** for 2023 service. It excludes
SeaBus, HandyDART, rail, non-revenue vehicles, brake/tire wear, fuel upstream,
electricity upstream, depot construction and vehicle manufacturing. The
all-revenue-fleet CAC series is not used because it cannot be decomposed into
fixed-route bus pollutants. No value from that aggregate series enters any
calculation below.

The total service distance is 106,868,324 km and service time is 5,575,772 h,
so the mean service speed is

`v = 106,868,324 / 5,575,772 = 19.1666 km/h`.

The route-capacity proxy identifies the lower and upper non-trolley distance
fractions as 0.894234 and 0.897344. The central share is 0.895789. These are
trolley **proxies**, not a vehicle-level propulsion register. The fuel split is
constructed as follows:

`K_fossil = 106,868,324 p_fossil`;

`K_gasoline shuttle = 10,920,849 + 2,732,670 = 13,653,519 km`;

`G_NG = 575,495 / 0.96 = 599,473.958 GJ`;

`K_CNG = G_NG x 1000 / 19.383552 = 30,926,940 km`;

`K_diesel+hybrid = K_fossil - K_gasoline shuttle - K_CNG`;

`K_diesel = K_residual x 448 / (448 + 446 + 46)`;

`K_hybrid = K_residual - K_diesel`.

The EEA/COPERT hot-emission equation used for the Euro VI D/E checks is

`EF(v) = ((a v^2 + b v + c + d/v) / (e v^2 + z v + h)) (1 - RF)`.

The CNG energy factor is 19.383552 MJ/km at 50% load, 0% slope and 19.1666 km/h.
The CNG activity input is the 2023 bus natural-gas purchase, not a stock-share
guess. The residual diesel/hybrid split is a stock proxy; the 2022 snapshot has
448 low-emission diesel buses and 492 diesel-hybrid buses (446 CMBC plus 46
contracted). Community and contract shuttles are assigned to gasoline.

The existing transition output's 88.79 million non-deck fossil-km estimate is
retained only as a cross-check. It excludes double-deck blocks, so it is not used
as the total fixed-route activity denominator for this air-quality layer. Using
the full non-trolley proxy avoids silently dropping those bus-km.
The small number of battery-electric buses in service in 2023 are not separately
identifiable in the service-km feed; assigning all non-trolley distance to fossil
fuels is therefore a small conservative bias in the High/Central activity proxy.

Units are g/vehicle-km. The Low values are the EEA/COPERT Euro VI D/E urban
bus factors evaluated at the observed mean speed, except for gasoline shuttles,
where the available BC source is legacy. The Central heavy-duty values are the
real-world low-speed Euro VI Paris estimates of about 2.0 g/km diesel, 1.4 g/km
hybrid and 0.6 g/km CNG; this source does not provide PM mass, so the Central PM
entries are arithmetic midpoints between the EEA and BC sources. The High
diesel/hybrid and gasoline factors are the BC Ministry's legacy on-road bus
table. No BC-specific CNG bus factor was located, so CNG remains at the
measured low-speed value in all scenarios.

The EEA workbook defines the exhaust PM cell as PM2.5/PM10/TSP for this urban
bus class; the Low PM2.5 and PM10 columns are therefore equal. The BC legacy
rows preserve separate PM2.5 and PM10 columns. Hybrid PM is assigned the same
Euro VI diesel exhaust after-treatment factor because no separate hybrid PM
coefficient is provided in the selected EEA row.

### B.4 What remains unverified

- No 2023 vehicle-km or fuel record split by diesel, hybrid, CNG, trolley,
  battery-electric and gasoline shuttle was available. The fuel allocation is
  explicitly a proxy.
- The trolley distance is inferred from 47/70-seat block signatures; the GTFS
  feed does not certify propulsion.
- The gasoline shuttle factor is a BC legacy inventory factor, because no newer
  BC-specific gasoline bus factor was found.
- The EEA Euro VI and Paris real-world studies are not a Vancouver chassis or
  PEMS test; temperature, duty cycle, after-treatment condition and route grade
  are not calibrated to 2023 TransLink operations.
- The economic operating boundary is the published Bus Operations umbrella; a
  fixed-route-only cost separation would require an operator ledger by service
  group. The capital envelope is conditional and not committed spending.
- Final bus assembly location and the category-specific local procurement share
  are unknown. The 72.5% BC spend share is an enterprise-wide accounting ratio,
  not a bus-manufacturing local-content rate.
