# CPU score run: sourced 40-foot reference case

The executable calculation is `python src/compute_reference_scores.py`; machine-readable output is `results/cpu_reference_scores.json`. Run on CPU only. More compute cannot recover missing vehicle blocks, depot capacities or a 2023 class-by-age fleet register. The numbers below are a **reference case**, not a certified score for the entire Metro Vancouver 2024–2033 transition.

| Metric | Result | Boundary |
|---|---:|---|
| Economic score, diesel comparator | **−5.19%** | 40-ft diesel vs in-route BEB historical lifetime model, 2019 CAD/km; 4.62 vs 4.86 |
| Economic score, hybrid comparator | **−1.89%** | 40-ft hybrid vs in-route BEB, 2019 CAD/km; 4.77 vs 4.86 |
| Economic score, CNG comparator | **−17.11%** | 40-ft CNG vs in-route BEB, 2019 CAD/km; 4.15 vs 4.86 |
| Environmental score | **98.85%** | Same distance; operating diesel combustion versus electricity at BC's 2023 integrated-grid accounting average |
| Full life-cycle environmental score | **unavailable** | Missing matched vehicle, battery, infrastructure, upstream fuel and end-of-life emissions |
| Full-city 2024–2033 cost / environment scores | **unavailable** | No verified exact trips, fleet age/class/depot register, or charger/grid capacities |

The historical [TransLink fleet plan](https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/about-translink/policies/translink_low_carbon_fleet_transition_plan_2020_02_24.pdf), Appendix D, gives 2019-CAD lifetime costs of 4.62, 4.77, 4.15 and 4.86 CAD/km for diesel, hybrid, CNG and in-route charged BEB 40-ft buses respectively. The three economic scores above are separate renewal comparators; the actual comparator depends on which class and propulsion would have replaced each retiring bus. Its in-route BEB scenario uses 1.6 kWh/km, with no fuel heater in the input table. The separate depot scenario uses diesel heat; its 5.14 CAD/km figure is **not** used as an eligible all-electric option. The lifetime cost values are source model outputs, not independently recomputed discounted 2024–2033 cash flows. The [Dec 2020 update](https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/media-releases/2021/january/translink_low_carbon_fleet_transition_plan_update_2021_01_18.pdf) explicitly compares an electrification purchase program against ordinary replacement and shows that normal renewal does not mean one uniform diesel counterfactual.

The [2024 ZEFTP](https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/about-translink/governance-and-board/council-minutes-and-reports/2024/november/agenda--mayors-council--public-mtg--nov-28-2024.pdf), Attachment 3, gives retrospective **operation and maintenance only** costs in 2024 CAD/vehicle-km: diesel 3.30, hybrid 1.97, CNG 2.77 and BEB 2.28. The corresponding BEB O&M scores are +30.91%, −15.74% and +17.69%. They omit bus and charging capital and therefore cannot be used as whole-project economic scores or combined arithmetically with the 2019-CAD lifetime figures.

For the operating GHG comparison, use TransLink's 0.60 L/km and 1.6 kWh/km, the federal [Zero Emission Transit Fund GHG module](https://housing-infrastructure.canada.ca/zero-emissions-trans-zero-emissions/ghg-modules-ges-eng.html) Annex D **heavy-duty** diesel B4 combustion factor of 2.630 kg CO2e/L, and [BC's 2023 integrated-grid factor](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/climate-change/data/electricity) of 11.3 t CO2e/GWh = 0.0113 kg/kWh. Thus diesel is 1.578 kg/km and BEB electricity is 0.01808 kg/km. These scope choices exclude diesel fuel production and manufacturing in both cases. The BC factor is an annual accounting average, not marginal generation.

At the source's 68,000 km/year, this is 107.304 t/year versus 1.22944 t/year, or 106.07456 t/year operating savings per equivalent bus. This is a matched-distance arithmetic benchmark; it does **not** establish that one BEB can cover the same daily block, peak payload or charger schedule as one diesel bus.

Dividing the historical 0.24 CAD/km extra lifetime cost by the 1.55992 kg/km operating GHG reduction gives **153.85 2019-CAD per operating tonne avoided**. This is a marginal comparison of the two source model outputs, not a full project cost-effectiveness estimate.

Holding every other term of the historical lifetime-cost model fixed, diesel would need to rise from 1.04 to 1.44 CAD/L, while BEB power stays at 0.10 CAD/kWh, for cost parity. This is a **sensitivity threshold in 2019 dollars**, not a price forecast. At 2023 BC grid intensity and the plan's 1.37–2.00 kWh/km annual 40-ft energy range, the operating-GHG reduction is 99.02–98.57%. Replacing the 2023 grid average with the historical 2020 integrated-grid factor gives 96.52–94.92%. These are paired parameter cases, not statistical confidence intervals. The detailed 3-by-3 grid is in the JSON.

The [2023 TransLink accountability report](https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/about-translink/corporate-reports/accountability_reports/2023/accountability_report_2023.pdf) also reports 100, 23 and 2 g CO2e/passenger-km for diesel, natural-gas and battery-electric bus modes respectively. Direct intensity ratios give 98.0% for diesel-to-BEB and 91.30% for natural-gas-to-BEB, but these have actual occupancy and renewable-gas accounting embedded in them. They are not interchangeable with the same-distance bus energy model above and cannot be applied across the full fleet without its vehicle and service mix. The 2024 ZEFTP gives a distinct matched-vehicle-km comparison: 19 g/km for BEB with electric heat versus 1580 fossil diesel, 1420 fossil hybrid, 1350 fossil CNG or 250 renewable CNG. This makes the counterfactual fuel mix decisive for the environmental score too; those values remain operational rather than life-cycle.

The 2023 baseline is now partially anchored by TransLink's [December 2023 report of 280 trolley plus battery-electric buses](https://www.translink.ca/news/2023/december/metro%20vancouver%20diesel%20buses%20transitioning%20to%20cleaner%20renewable%20fuel), a [February count of 262 trolleys](https://www.translink.ca/news/2023/february/translink%20begins%20battery-electric%20bus%20fleet%20expansion), and 299 CNG vehicles in the accountability report. The same-day official Buzzer article instead describes approximately 275 trolleys and five BEBs. Subtracting the February trolley count to infer 18 December BEBs crosses dates and conflicts with that article; it is not an accepted December stock count. The next numerical gate is still a 2023 fixed-route trip/block manifest, vehicle class/age/depot register and depot power/space/charger inventory. The outstanding fields are listed in `research/data_request.md`. Only after those are supplied can the hard service constraints and the paper's discounted 2024–2033 and full life-cycle scores be computed honestly.

The separate `results/capital_cohort_audit.json` reproduces partial retirement projects from the [2025 business plan](https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/about-translink/corporate-reports/business_plan/2025-business-plan-operating-and-capital-budget-summary.pdf). Its strongest feasibility stress is the 2026 program for 45 depot-charged articulated BEBs replacing 39 diesels. Applying [TransLink's historical depot replacement ratios](https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/about-translink/policies/translink_low_carbon_fleet_transition_plan_2020_02_24.pdf), Appendix C Table C-2, and its approximate 10% electric-only cabin-heat penalty produces 49–67 buses across hypothetical single-depot allocations, 4–22 above that capital-program count. This is an old-technology *sensitivity*, not a demonstrated procurement shortfall; neither the depot assignment nor the 2026 vehicle's winter performance is available.

## Energy-price and loss-boundary update

The official April-2023 BC Hydro tariff and original 2023 diesel budget price are now digitized. The new `charging_tariff_bounds.json` uses an explicit battery-energy/charging-efficiency convention, actual demand-charge windows and invoice floors. The earlier 98.85% operating-emission arithmetic above uses 1.6 kWh/km directly without a charging-loss uplift; it remains that narrower source-parameter reference, not a meter-energy or full-life-cycle score. See `research/charging_tariff_note.md` for the independently solved power/billing thresholds and exclusions.
